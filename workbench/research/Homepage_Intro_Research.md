# Homepage Intro Research

## Purpose
Collect useful options for the homepage/about introduction before writing new copy.

## Current Read
The intro needs to hold several real threads without flattening them into a tidy role summary:

| Thread | What It Might Carry | Watch Out For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product and strategy work | operating judgment, systems, technology, company-building, practical taste | sounding like a LinkedIn summary |
| Ghosts of Bombay | food, memory, migration, hospitality, authorship, scene-making | turning the intro into a manifesto |
| Detroit | local life, gathering, hospitality, current community | stale civic-brand language |
| Writing and research | public notes, essays, open questions, fieldwork | generic "I write about..." phrasing |
| Personal site as working surface | a place for writing, projects, experiments, archives | process language that belongs in the repo, not the page |

## Intro Angles To Consider
| Angle | Shape | Useful Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Workbench intro | "I work on X, write about Y, and host/build Z." | What are the few nouns that feel alive enough to sit next to each other? |
| Current obsessions | Starts from themes rather than job title. | Which themes would still feel true in two years? |
| Split practice | Names product work and Ghosts of Bombay as parallel practices. | Are these two worlds separate, or part of the same taste for systems, scenes, and hospitality? |
| Local life | Detroit as the ground the work happens from. | Does place belong in the first sentence or the second paragraph? |
| Archive plus live signal | Old writing remains visible while the intro points forward. | How do we make the site feel alive without promising frequent posting? |

## Pattern Map
| Pattern | What It Foregrounds | When It Works | Risk | Rahul Question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Workbench intro | active questions, current work, what is being built or studied | the site should feel alive even with occasional updates | sounding like a status page | What are the 2-3 live inquiries that make your work feel current? |
| Intellectual territory map | recurring domains: product, food, cities, Bombay, Detroit, systems, culture | one professional label is too narrow | becoming an "interests include" list | Which territories keep returning even when the project changes? |
| "I make X" intro | outputs: essays, product work, dinners, notes, experiments | the homepage should route people toward artifacts | flattening the person into productivity language | Which outputs should become legible first? |
| Bespoke notebook intro | personal notebook, partial archive, publishing surface | writing will be irregular but intentional | apologizing for incompleteness | Should the site feel like a finished profile or a working surface? |
| Life-throughline intro | the bridge from strategy consulting to product to food/hospitality/culture | biography explains the mix of topics | LinkedIn "journey" language | Is the throughline taste, systems, cities, gathering, or translation between worlds? |
| Project constellation intro | a few named projects or containers, with Ghosts of Bombay visible | multiple semi-independent lines of work need a home | portfolio-grid energy | What belongs in the first constellation: Ghosts of Bombay, essays, product work, Detroit/Bombay, fieldwork? |
| High-signal minimal intro | name, current role, one orienting sentence, then links | design and archive carry most of the personality | too thin to explain the mix | How little can the homepage say while still making product and Ghosts of Bombay feel native to one person? |
| Invitation intro | the reader/collaborator/diner/curious stranger being welcomed | site is part public commons and part calling card | audience-growth copy | Who should feel quietly invited here? |

## Open Questions
- Should the homepage use first person, or should first person live only on About?
- Should Ghosts of Bombay be named immediately, or introduced after the broader frame?
- Should the product/strategy thread use company language, craft language, or project language?
- What does Rahul want a stranger to understand in ten seconds?
- What does Rahul want a friend to recognize as true?

## Source Notes
- Michael Nielsen's homepage leads with an "online notebook" identity, then uses a dense link-rich intro to hold current projects, past work, books, selected projects, and tags.
- Andy Matuschak leads with a crisp working identity, expands into current focus and belief, then locates the work through funding context and prior roles.
- Interconnected shows the value of letting dated posts and old-web navigation carry the archive without over-framing each page.
- Ribbonfarm and Venkatesh Rao are useful for ecosystem thinking: archive, current projects, consulting/writing, and side worlds can be separate surfaces.
